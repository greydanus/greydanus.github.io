## About me

I'm interested in how neural networks learn and generalize: interpretability and the science of deep learning and AI for science. I wrote [Hamiltonian Neural Networks](../2019/05/15/hamiltonian-nns/) ([2k+](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=hamiltonian+neural+networks&oq=hamiltonian+neural+) citations; NeurIPS 2019) and co-authored [Lagrangian Neural Networks](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=lagrangian+neural+networks) (with Miles Cranmer). I've also worked with Stephan Hoyer on [neural reparameterization for structural optimization](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=neural+reparameterization+structural+optimization), Chris Olah on [the paths perspective on value learning](https://distill.pub/2019/paths-perspective-on-value-learning/), and Dmitry Kobak on [MNIST-1D](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=scaling+down+with+mnist1d) (ICML 2024).

Lately I've been studying how transformers reason and generalize on data with 2D structure (think tables, algorithms, and some coding tasks). I'm also training [pen ink models](https://github.com/greydanus/pengpt) that sketch images and write realistic handwriting in a variety of styles.

I'm the Lead AI Scientist at [Humata Health](https://www.humatahealth.com/) (acquired by [R1](https://www.r1rcm.com/)) and a research collaborator at [General Intuition](https://www.generalintuition.com/). Previously I worked at Google Brain, DARPA's Explainable AI Program and CERN, and studied physics at Dartmouth College. This is my research blog, where I write about ideas that interest me and highlight papers I've written.

* 2013 – Physics major, Dartmouth College
* 2015 – Particle physics intern, CERN
* 2016 – Software engineering intern, Microsoft
* 2017 – Co-captain of US team, International Physicists' Tournament

* 2024 – Lead AI Scientist, [Humata Health](https://www.humatahealth.com/)
* 2026 – Research collaborator, [General Intuition](https://www.generalintuition.com/)

## Selected papers

* [Hamiltonian Neural Networks](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=hamiltonian+neural+networks&oq=hamiltonian+neural+). Networks that learn to conserve energy from data (NeurIPS 2019).
* [Lagrangian Neural Networks](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=lagrangian+neural+networks). Learns arbitrary Lagrangians without canonical coordinates (ICLR 2020 workshop).
* [Visualizing and Understanding Atari Agents](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=visualizing+understanding+atari+agents). Saliency maps that explain deep RL agents (ICML 2018).
* [Scaling Down Deep Learning with MNIST-1D](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=scaling+down+with+mnist1d). A tiny benchmark for the science of deep learning (ICML 2024).
* [Neural Reparameterization Improves Structural Optimization](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C13&q=neural+reparameterization+structural+optimization). Designs parameterized by a neural network come out stronger (NeurIPS 2019 workshop).
* [The Paths Perspective on Value Learning](https://distill.pub/2019/paths-perspective-on-value-learning/). Why temporal-difference learning works, shown visually (Distill 2019).

## Older projects

* [Taming wave functions with neural networks](https://quantumfrontiers.com/2017/08/02/taming-wave-functions-with-neural-networks/). My undergraduate [thesis](/files/greydanus-dartmouth-thesis.pdf) with James Whitfield, written up for Caltech's quantum blog (2017).
* [Higgs electroweak calibration](/cern.html). A semester at CERN simulating what happens to Higgs physics when you leave out the electroweak force (2015).
* [Machine learning for fMRI](/cosan.html). Comparing searchlight analysis with machine learning decoders, with Luke Chang ([poster](/files/cosan_searchlight_poster.pdf)).
* [Nearshore physics](/nearshore.html). Tracking internal waves in time-lapse footage of the Columbia River estuary ([AGU 2014 talk](/files/greydanus_holman.pdf)).
* [Stereograms](https://github.com/greydanus/stereograms). I got so excited about depth perception that I wrote code to make my own, and an article for the Dartmouth Undergraduate Journal of Science.
* [Fractal tree](https://greydanus.github.io/fractal_tree/index.html) and [friendly Q-learning](https://greydanus.github.io/friendly_qlearning/index.html). Two browser toys: a tree blowing in the wind and agents that learn to get along.
* [Baby A3C](https://github.com/greydanus/baby-a3c). An Atari agent in 180 lines of PyTorch.
* I did a Sudoku on a plane and then decided to [solve the general case](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rcxxweV8VRzVj7nJtADkGmtX1G6hnRIi/view?usp=sharing).
